TrailDesk, an app for finding a public place you can actually work from

70% of remote workers face difficulties working from home. No one could tell them where else to go.

TrailDesk

Platform
workspace discovery app
Role
UX/UI · Branding
Process

Tools

The problem

Everyone wants to get out of the house. Nobody knows where to go.

What people run into

Most remote workers face difficulties working from home because of noise, distractions, and a lack of separation between work and personal life.

Many want to work outside the home but avoid it because of uncertainty about the conditions in public workspaces. There is no solution that aggregates a wide range of public spaces with reliable, up-to-date information on what they are actually like.

Main goal

TrailDesk's main goal is to provide a platform that aggregates a wide range of public workspaces with reliable, up-to-date information on their conditions — including comfortable seating, Wi-Fi quality, power availability, noise levels, parking, and more.

Choosing where to work should be a decision, not a gamble.

User research

Three interviews, and the same loop described three times

To make sure TrailDesk solved a problem people actually have, I ran in-depth qualitative research with remote workers — three long-form interviews, a survey, and user-journey mapping. The three people worked in different fields and on different schedules, and still described the same loop back to me.

01 Going out is a gamble

Wi-Fi, power outlets and noise are unknown until you are already sitting there. When the guess is wrong the trip is wasted, and so is the flow it took to get going.

02 The search itself costs hours

One interviewee spends about a fifth of his workday on logistics — checking the Wi-Fi, finding a socket, moving on. For another, "location hunting" stretches an eight-hour day into ten.

03 Home never switches off

Interruptions push work into the evening, so the day runs long and the line between work and personal life almost disappears.

82%

often seek a change in their work location to feel more productive and creative

74%

take intentional breaks very often to cope with distractions

68%

don't use any platform to find public remote workspaces, because so little is available

59%

ask friends or search social media to find a public workspace instead

Market overview

Everything on the market points at the same two answers

I ran in-depth market and competitive research with AI-assisted desk research. The existing products all lead to the same two places: a very expensive coworking space, or a generic coffee shop. None of them cover the free, public, in-between places people already work from — a library, a park, a quiet lobby.

The gap wasn't a missing place to work. It was missing information about the places that already exist.

The solution

And this is how TrailDesk was born.

A map-first app that puts every public workspace nearby on one screen, and grades each one on the things that actually decide whether you can work there — before you pack up and go.

User journey

From "I can't focus" to "I can help someone else find this"

Mapping the journey is what turned the interviews into a product: the low point isn't the work, it's the not knowing — and the mood only climbs once a decision can be made with real information.

Information architecture

One home screen, four tabs, nothing deeper than it needs to be

Everything after onboarding hangs off a single home screen — the map. Categories, Favorites, Messages and Profile each run at most three levels down, so you are never more than a couple of taps from the map you came from.

User flow

The path to a place you'll actually get up and go to

The main flow is a filter-and-decide loop. If the filtered list holds nothing, it goes straight back to the filter rather than back to the start. And when someone hesitates over a place, the flow doesn't dead-end — it opens the reviews, and from there a direct message to a member who has already been there.

UI design

A map you can read at a glance, and a card that answers before you scroll

The map opens first, with every category colour-coded as its own pin and the filter row sitting on top of it. Tapping a place opens a card that leads with a photo and then, immediately, the conditions scored out of ten — Wi-Fi, power, noise, seating, parking — with walking and driving time and a single Navigate button underneath. The whole decision is made above the fold, which is the entire point of the product.

Trail Desk
Impact

Choosing where to work stops being a gamble and becomes a decision.

The information people were missing already existed — it was just never gathered anywhere. TrailDesk puts it on one map, scores it on the five things that decide whether a place works, and lets the people who have already been there answer the rest.
